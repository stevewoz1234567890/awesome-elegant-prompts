## Real-world applications: needs

Here is a simple way to tie ideas together: start from a **need**, sketch what you might **ask** an LLM, then **refine** with constraints (format, tone, length, audience, exclusions).

Below are three scenarios. Each states the situation first—the kind of context you would paste or summarize in a real prompt.

### School use

You are taking a calculus class and you are stuck on a problem set about integrals. It has been a few years since you last took a math class, and you are having trouble remembering integration techniques for different kinds of functions.

### Professional use

You are up for promotion and you need to write a self-review. You have notes on what you accomplished this year, but it does not feel like enough, and you are unsure what tone to strike.

### Personal use

You are hosting a dinner party for friends you have not seen in a long time. You like cooking but do not have much experience. Some guests have different dietary needs, and a couple of young children will be there—picky eaters.

## Real-world applications: first drafts

You know what you need, so the next step is a **first draft** of a prompt: plain language, good enough to paste and iterate. These are not polished—they omit details you would still add (the actual integral, your accomplishment bullets, dietary lists)—but they show the shape of a natural ask.

### School use (first draft)

```text
I don't get how to do this integration problem, can you show me how?
```

### Professional use (first draft)

```text
I need to write a self-performance review. Here are a few things I've accomplished, can you make them into a review for me?
```

### Personal use (first draft)

```text
What are some easy things I can make for a dinner party? I've got ten people coming, and they all like different things.
```

---

**Refining:** paste the missing context (problem statement, notes, restrictions), then layer **constraints**—length, tone, format, audience, what to avoid—so the model’s output matches how you will actually use it.
